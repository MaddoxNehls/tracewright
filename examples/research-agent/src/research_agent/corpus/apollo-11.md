# Apollo 11

Apollo 11 was the first mission to land humans on the Moon. It launched on July 16, 1969, with Neil Armstrong (commander), Buzz Aldrin (lunar module pilot) and Michael Collins (command module pilot).

The lunar module Eagle landed in the Sea of Tranquility on July 20, 1969, at 20:17 UTC. Armstrong became the first person to walk on the Moon about six and a half hours later, saying "That's one small step for [a] man, one giant leap for mankind." Aldrin joined him about 20 minutes later, while Collins orbited alone in the command module Columbia.

The moonwalk lasted about two and a half hours, and the crew collected 21.5 kilograms of lunar samples. Apollo 11 splashed down in the Pacific Ocean on July 24, 1969, fulfilling President Kennedy's 1961 goal of a landing before the decade was out.
