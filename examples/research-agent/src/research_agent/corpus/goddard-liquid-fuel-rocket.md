# Robert Goddard's Liquid-Fuel Rocket

On March 16, 1926, the American physicist Robert H. Goddard launched the world's first liquid-fueled rocket from a farm in Auburn, Massachusetts. It burned gasoline and liquid oxygen.

The flight lasted about 2.5 seconds, climbed roughly 12.5 meters (41 feet), and landed about 56 meters (184 feet) away. Modest as it was, it proved that liquid propellants could be throttled and controlled in ways solid powder rockets could not.

Goddard went on to develop gyroscopic stabilization and movable exhaust vanes for steering. He received little public recognition during his life, and a 1920 New York Times editorial mocked his claim that rockets could work in a vacuum. The paper printed a correction in July 1969, as Apollo 11 flew to the Moon.
