# Tsiolkovsky and the Rocket Equation

Konstantin Tsiolkovsky was a Russian schoolteacher who laid the theoretical foundations of spaceflight. In 1903 he published "The Exploration of Cosmic Space by Means of Reaction Devices", which derived what is now called the Tsiolkovsky rocket equation.

The equation relates a rocket's change in velocity (delta-v) to its exhaust velocity and the ratio of its initial to final mass. It explains why reaching orbit is so hard: delta-v grows only with the logarithm of the mass ratio, so most of a launch vehicle's mass must be propellant.

Tsiolkovsky also proposed liquid hydrogen and liquid oxygen as propellants and described multistage rockets, an idea every orbital launcher later adopted. He died in 1935, before any rocket reached space.
